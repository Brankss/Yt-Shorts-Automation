# Strategy — Would You Rather

Everything here comes from data pulled on 2026-09-08 with the `youtube-*` skills:
16 niches researched (~5,400 videos), 1,001 comments mined from the breakout
videos of small channels, and a 30-day trending scan. Numbers are English-only
and conditioned on channel size — a niche's overall average is set by incumbents
whose floor sits above our ceiling, so it tells us nothing about what we can reach.

## Why this niche

| Signal | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Median views, channels <100K subs | **9,628** | Second highest of 16 niches tested |
| Small channels in results | **60 of 76** | The largest reachable surface of any niche |
| Breakouts from channels <10K subs | **6** | Unknown channels clear 100K here routinely |
| Rising channels right now | 29, including accounts at **22, 26, 27 subs** | The algorithm distributes on content, not on existing audience |
| Best small-channel result | **904,865 views from 937 subs** | The ceiling is not gated by subscriber count |

The competing option, psychology facts, has a higher median (234K) but is a
text-and-voiceover format where the only lever is hook writing. Would You Rather
is a *mechanic*, and a mechanic can be engineered, templated, and repeated.
Psychology stays in reserve as a second channel or a fallback.

## What the data says about the format

### Short does not work here

This is the finding that contradicts standard Shorts advice, and it is the single
most expensive mistake we could make:

| Duration | n | Median views |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15s | 3 | 20 |
| 16–25s | 8 | 751 |
| 26–40s | 25 | 14,422 |
| **41–60s** | **35** | **26,239** |
| 61–90s | 5 | 8,834 |

Nine-second videos win in psychology facts. Here they die. The mechanic needs
room: present the dilemma, let the viewer commit to a choice, then reveal. Cut
that short and there is no payoff, so there is no reason to watch or comment.

**Target duration: 45–55 seconds.**

### Food is the highest-converting sub-vertical

"Food edition" appears in the title of breakout videos at every channel size —
including the 324-subscriber channel that got 135,487 views and the 865-subscriber
channel that got 446,781. In the mined comments, food terms dominate the audience's
own vocabulary: `pizza` (77), `pineapple` (61), `chicken` (31), `fries` (28).

Food is universal, needs no setup, is visually trivial to render, and is
maximally polarizing at zero risk. It is the entry wedge.

### The percentage reveal is the engagement engine

The single most-liked comment across every breakout video mined:

> **"How tf is 90 % and 15% 💀💀💀"** — 148 likes

and close behind:

> "Love how half of these are misspelled and the numbers don't quite add up to 100%" — 49 likes
>
> "no way people picked bagels instead of donuts" — 22 likes
>
> "Guys stop commenting that the percentages don't match up" — 4 likes

People do not comment because a dilemma is interesting. They comment because a
number told them they are in the minority, or because the number was wrong.
The reveal is the product. The dilemma is just its delivery mechanism.

This cuts both ways, and it is why our percentages have to be **real** — see below.

### The three comment triggers, ranked

1. **Counterintuitive split.** The majority picks the option the viewer considers
   obviously wrong. `0:23 WHAT THE FU- FAST FOOD OVER HOME COOKED MEALS? 😨`
2. **"I already am that."** Dilemmas naming a condition the viewer already lives
   with produce instant self-deprecating replies. `I already have bad eyesight lol`
   (49 likes, and a near-duplicate at 18 likes on the same video).
3. **Broken edge case.** A dilemma that does not apply to the viewer.
   `What happens if I don't have a sister and bad hearing 😅` — 138 likes.

Trigger 3 is free engagement, but it costs credibility. Use 1 and 2 deliberately;
let 3 happen on its own.

### Audience vocabulary

From 1,001 mined comments, the words the audience actually uses:
`never` (103) · `pain` (79) · `pizza` (77) · `pineapple` (61) · `sister` (59) ·
`glow` (45) · `money` (45) · `unlimited` (38) · `already` (37) · `both` (36) ·
`cold` (36) · `single` (33) · `sadness` (31)

`never` and `unlimited` are the two framings that recur most: "would you rather
never X" and "unlimited Y". `both` is people refusing to choose — a signal the
dilemma was not sharp enough.

### Serialization works

The largest channel in the niche runs numbered episodes — `Would You Rather...? #9`
at 75M views, `#39` at 18.6M, `#43` at 15.1M. A 89,900-sub channel does the same
with `HARD EDITION! (Episode 5)`. And in the comments of one of them:

> "Which options did you pick? 10k likes for episode 6" — 62 likes

Episodes give the viewer a reason to come back and give us a reason to publish
daily without inventing a new concept each time.

## Our strategic edge: real percentages

Every channel in this niche invents its percentages. That is why viewers catch
numbers that do not add to 100 — and why the top comment on the biggest breakout
is people mocking the math.

Publishing invented survey results as if they were measured data is both
dishonest and, as the comments show, actively noticed. We do the opposite, and
turn it into the growth loop:

```
Episode N     asks the 5 dilemmas, and pins a comment linking a Community poll
              ↓
Community     real viewers vote on those exact 5 dilemmas
poll
              ↓
Episode N+1   opens with "here is what YOU picked" and reveals the REAL numbers,
              then asks 5 new dilemmas
```

This gives us four things no competitor has:

1. **Numbers that are true**, and that survive scrutiny in the comments.
2. **A reason to return** — you voted, you want to see if you were in the majority.
3. **Community-tab activity**, which YouTube counts as channel engagement.
4. **A free content well** — the poll results *are* the next episode's hook.

Until the channel has enough voters for the poll to mean anything, episodes state
the split as an explicit prediction ("most people pick…") with no fabricated
number, and the first real percentages land once polls have volume.

## Positioning

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | 5 dilemmas per episode, numbered, 45–55s |
| **Entry vertical** | Food (episodes 1–20), then widen to money / pain / relationships |
| **Voice** | Fast, dry, slightly provocative. Never explains the joke. |
| **Difficulty ramp** | Dilemma 1 easy and funny → dilemma 5 genuinely hard |
| **Hook** | Dilemma 1 *is* the hook. No intro, no "welcome back". First frame is a choice. |
| **CTA** | One line, at the reveal of dilemma 5, pointing at the poll |

## What we are deliberately not doing

- **No sub-25s cuts.** The data says they collect nothing.
- **No borrowed IP.** The niche's biggest velocity outlier is a Minecraft-skinned
  WYR at 436K views/day, and anime/game reskins run through the whole niche. It
  works, and it is someone else's asset. Our motion design has to carry it.
- **No fabricated statistics.** Covered above.
- **No face, no voice actor.** Everything must stay renderable from HTML so the
  pipeline can run unattended.
